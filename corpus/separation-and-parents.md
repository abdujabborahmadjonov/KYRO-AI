# Separation Anxiety and Parental Presence

Fear of being separated from a parent is one of the most common worries for children facing medical procedures — often bigger than fear of the procedure itself. Telling the child exactly when a parent will be present, and where the parent will be waiting during any separation, reduces anxiety more than general reassurance.

For most imaging (X-ray, MRI, CT), a parent can remain in the room or immediately outside with a window or intercom. For procedures with anesthesia, many hospitals allow a parent to stay until the child is asleep and to be present in the recovery room as the child wakes up.

Parents' own anxiety transfers to children. Children read a parent's face and voice; calm, matter-of-fact parents have calmer children. Preparing parents with clear information — what will happen, how long it takes, what it costs — is part of preparing the child.

Bringing a comfort item from home — a stuffed animal, a favorite blanket — is encouraged for most procedures. Some MRI centers even "scan" the stuffed animal first to show the child what happens. The comfort item should be checked for metal parts before entering an MRI room.
