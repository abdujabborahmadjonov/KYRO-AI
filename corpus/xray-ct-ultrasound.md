# X-rays, CT Scans, and Ultrasounds for Children

An X-ray is a fast picture — usually done in seconds. It doesn't hurt and nothing touches the child except the cool table or the board they stand against. The child's job is to stay still for a few seconds, sometimes holding a big breath "like blowing up an invisible balloon."

A CT scan is like a camera shaped like a big donut that spins around to take pictures. It is much faster than an MRI — often under a minute of actual scanning — and much quieter. The table moves slowly through the donut hole. The machine never touches the child.

An ultrasound uses a small wand and warm jelly on the skin to see inside the body — the same machine used to see babies before they're born. It doesn't hurt; the jelly might feel funny and a little cold at first. The lights are usually dimmed so the pictures are easier to see, like a movie theater.

For any imaging, technologists can always see and hear the child, explain each step before it happens, and pause if the child needs a moment. Children cope best when each step is announced right before it occurs ("now the table is going to move — it feels like a slow ride").
